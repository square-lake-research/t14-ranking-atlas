Definitions and source notes

  1. Employment and placements: ABA official Class of 2025 compilation (Excel), downloaded September 17, 2026, from the ABA Required Disclosures portal. Snapshot as of March 16, 2026, about ten months after graduation. Every percentage in the first five outcome columns uses all graduates as its denominator; the displayed count shows the numerator and denominator. Percentages are arithmetic from this one dataset, rounded to one decimal place.
  2. Overall employment: all employed job categories, including full/part-time and long/short-term work and school-funded positions. Further study and deferred job starts are excluded. It is not the narrower full-time, long-term bar-required/JD-advantage employment metric.
  3. Large firms: full-time, long-term jobs at firms with 101 or more attorneys; the adjacent 501+ column provides a stricter size measure. Firm size is not the National Law Journal's top-100-firm definition and does not certify prestige or market-rate pay.
  4. Clerkships: full-time, long-term judicial clerkships, including federal, state/local/territorial, tribal and international. Federal clerkships are a subset of all clerkships; do not add these two columns. These are immediate outcomes, not eventual or lifetime clerkship placement.
  5. Starting salaries: directly published school salary disclosures or school-published NALP summaries. Each row links the selected primary source and states the class, scope, statistic and available coverage. All-sector median and sector statistic are separated. Yale's figure is a mean, not a median. Full-time versus full-time/long-term definitions differ as noted. Bonuses are not added. Salary disclosure samples may differ from ABA employment totals. Missing medians were not calculated from sector medians.
  6. Later earnings: U.S. Department of Education College Scorecard June 10, 2026 field-of-study release, the latest release listed on its official data page. Selected Law (CIP 2201), First Professional Degree (credential level 7), by each institution's UNITID; values are the raw EARN_MDN_4YR field, without recalculation. The official data dictionary and cohort map identifies pooled 2017–18 and 2018–19 completers, measured in calendar 2022 and 2023, inflation-adjusted to 2024 dollars. This is a different, older cohort from Class of 2025.
  7. Earnings coverage: federal-aid recipients working and not enrolled four years after completion, not all graduates and not necessarily lawyers currently practicing. The official field-of-study technical documentation describes IRS W-2 earnings plus positive self-employment earnings, privacy protection and aid-recipient coverage. Sample counts come directly from EARN_COUNT_WNE_4YR and are privacy-protected. UCLA's published count is only 24, so its median deserves particular caution. These are annual earnings, not base starting salaries or estimates of lifetime earning potential.
  8. Missing values: “Privacy suppressed” reproduces the federal source's PS status. “Not verified” means no sufficiently recent numeric primary-source figure could be verified in this research; it does not mean zero or that no data exists. “Not disclosed” means the selected cited salary source does not publish that overall median. No older earnings value, general BigLaw salary scale, modeled estimate or number assembled across sources is substituted. There are no composite rankings.
